# Archetype: Lands

## Strategic constraints

1. Establish the Life from the Loam recursion engine
2. Use Wasteland recursion to grind the opponent's land base to nothing
3. Win via Dark Depths + Thespian's Stage combo (instant-speed 20/20) or through pure resource attrition
4. Use Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale and Rishadan Port as additional pressure

## Permission profile

**Recursive and compounding — the most durable permission structure in Legacy.**

Wasteland + Life from the Loam is functionally unlimited denial. Each Loam activation returns Wasteland (and other lands) to hand. The opponent cannot permanently answer this with removal: Abrupt Decay destroys Blood Moon, but there is no equivalent that permanently stops Wasteland from recurring. The only answers are graveyard hate (Surgical Extraction, Leyline of the Void targeting Loam) or winning before it matters.

The permission gets stronger over time, not weaker. The longer the game goes:
- More Wasteland activations have resolved
- Opponent has fewer lands left
- Loam is generating more net advantage per activation as the graveyard fills

Compare to FoW (one-time, stable) and Red Prison locks (permanent but answerable): Lands' disruption is permanent and self-restoring.

## Time profile

**Always an asset. Slow games are winning games.**

This is the sharpest contrast with every other archetype in this taxonomy:
- Delver: time is roughly neutral (FoW stable, Daze bleeds, card disadvantage accumulates)
- Red Prison: asset after setup
- Oops: pure liability
- **Lands: time is always an asset, no ceiling**

The deck is designed for inevitability, not speed. The longer the game, the more Wasteland recursions, the more Loam card advantage, the more dead cards in the opponent's hand.

## The combo as insurance policy

Dark Depths + Thespian's Stage (Crop Rotation-fetchable, instant-speed) creates a 20/20 indestructible token. This is not the primary game plan — it's a release valve.

When the grind is working, you don't need it. When the opponent is about to stabilize despite the mana denial, Crop Rotation fetches Dark Depths and ends it immediately. The combo exists to punish opponents who survive long enough to stabilize, closing the game at exactly the moment the prison would otherwise be escapable.

This two-mode structure (grind OR instant combo kill) makes the deck difficult to hate out: hate the combo and lose to the grind; race the grind and lose to the combo.

## Resource axis attacked

Opponent's land base. Legacy manabases are built on ABUR dual lands with 0–2 basics. Wasteland recursion is a permanent, iterating denial of colored mana. The Tabernacle at Pendrell Vale attacks creature-based strategies separately — creatures in play must be paid for or die, taxing creature decks at a fundamental level.

## Cost structure

Lands has the most favorable resource profile of any archetype:
- No life cost (no Ancient Tomb)
- No card disadvantage on denial (Loam replaces itself through dredge)
- No reactive permission consuming cards

The cost is **opportunity cost on draw steps**: Life from the Loam requires dredging instead of drawing normally. Early in the game, before Loam is established, the deck can be slow and passive. Setup time is the main cost.

## Vulnerability

**Fast combo that wins before the mana denial matters.** Storm, Reanimator, Show and Tell — anything that can win on turns 1–2 before the engine is established. Lands has limited reactive permission to answer these.

Crop Rotation into Bojuka Bog handles some graveyard combo, but a turn-1 kill has essentially no answer in the main deck. This is the primary liability.

Graveyard hate (Surgical Extraction on Loam) can strand the recursion engine. Without Loam recurring, Wasteland is just a one-shot land destruction spell — still good but not inevitability.

## What a new card needs to fit here

One of:
- An additional recursion target (another land that provides repeated value when returned to hand by Loam)
- A faster or more resilient combo win condition (replaces or supplements Dark Depths)
- Reactive permission that doesn't require mana or cards — the main gap in the archetype is early-game answers to fast combo
- Additional mana denial axes (the current suite hits lands and creatures; a new axis would be valuable)

Cards providing incremental card advantage, reactive permission with costs, or speed-optimized threats are not what this archetype needs — it already wins the long game and just needs to not die before that.
